## Where the Council Agrees

Every advisor — and every peer review — converges on the same core diagnosis: **the central thesis, as stated, outruns the evidence.** "Topology adds predictive value" is written as a general finding but is carried by one dataset (banksim) out of three valid real ones, with ibm_aml's lift (+0.00792) smaller than the paper's own admitted noise floor (~0.01) and sap_wurzburg negative on n=25 frauds — a sample size too small for a single seed to mean anything. Strip synth_erp (correctly barred) and the "multi-dataset" thesis is really an n=1 positive result dressed as a general claim.

There is also unanimous agreement that **the statistics will not survive IEEE/Springer review as-is.** A single-seed, 7-arm × 4-dataset ablation with an informal noise-floor heuristic instead of bootstrap CIs or significance tests is a standard, one-sentence desk-reject vector. The mitigating fact everyone (especially the Executor) points to: the paper already runs a rigorous 3-seed harness for the TGN/focal-loss study, so extending it to the 7-arm ablation is an engineering rerun, not a redesign.

Every peer-review pass, independently, flagged the same cross-study contradiction as the sharpest point in the entire set (originating with the Contrarian): the paper's one interpretable, novel contribution — cycle-detection "shadow ledger" motifs — scores ~0 gain importance on 3 of 4 datasets under the *rigorous* 3-seed study, while the *unrigorous* single-seed ablation is what carries the abstract. Rigor was spent on the wrong question.

Finally, all five advisors treat the honest reporting of negative results and the two reproducibility findings as a genuine credibility asset — but a fragile one, contingent on the core statistics being fixed first. Disclosure without rigor reads as candor about a problem, not evidence the problem is solved.

## Where the Council Clashes

The real split is **fix vs. reframe.** The Contrarian, Executor, and Outsider hold that the paper's job right now is narrow and mechanical: rerun the ablation with 5+ seeds, add CIs, restate the thesis as conditional ("topology helps on 1–2 of 3 valid real datasets"), and stop there. The Expansionist and First Principles Thinker argue the mixed, dataset-conditional results are themselves a more interesting and more publishable finding — a "phase diagram" of when topology helps — and recommend restructuring, demoting the universal thesis, and even splitting the paper into three (ablation, negative-results/reproducibility study, systems paper) to maximize citable surface area.

This is not a minor stylistic disagreement, and peer review did not treat it as one: all five reviewers, independently and without collusion, named the Expansionist's response the weakest — for never engaging (a)–(e), for treating a statistical validity gap as a marketing opportunity, and for proposing to multiply submissions on top of an unfalsified core claim. The council's verdict on this clash is lopsided, not a coin flip: reframing is a legitimate second step, but proposed as a *first* step it is evasion, not strategy.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught

Peer review surfaced findings none of the five advisors reached on their own:

- **The TGN irreproducibility swing (0.938→0.845, Δ0.093) is almost exactly the size of banksim's headline lift (+0.097)** — the one dataset propping up the entire thesis. Nobody in the first round asked the obvious question: has anyone rerun banksim on a second machine? If hardware noise alone can produce a swing this size, the central claim may currently be indistinguishable from a reproducibility artifact, not a real effect.
- **The "rigorous" 3-seed study's own trustworthiness was never questioned.** If TGN training isn't bit-reproducible across machines, the 3-seed findings used to *discredit* the ablation (cycle-detection ≈0 importance, TGN never beating topology) need their own cross-machine check before being treated as the more-credible counterweight.
- **Internal validity, not just disclosed skew:** nobody in round one asked whether the catastrophic-cancellation bug (0.21% of rows) contaminated the *training and evaluation* data feeding the ablation itself — as opposed to being merely a documented train/serve skew in the new serving path. If it touched training data, it's a threat to every number in the paper, not a footnote.
- **Single-seed skepticism was never turned on the systems chapter.** The k6 duplicate-payment result ("exactly 1 success") is a single trial, and the load-test latency curves may be too — the same statistical standard the council demands of the ML ablation was never applied to the production claims.
- **No one flagged the multiple-comparisons problem.** Seven arms across four datasets on a single seed isn't just "missing CIs" — it's a multiple-testing setup with no correction, which independently inflates the odds that banksim's "win" is a false positive.
- **The forensic console's empirical justification was never questioned.** If cycle-detection scores ~0 gain importance on 3 of 4 real datasets, the production system built around "shadow ledger" motifs may be showcasing a feature that doesn't actually predict fraud.

## The Recommendation

Do not submit as-is, and do not restructure into multiple papers yet — that decision is premature until you know whether you have one real result or zero. The council's majority (Contrarian, Executor, Outsider, First Principles Thinker on the statistics question) is right that this is fundamentally a rigor problem, not a framing problem, and the Expansionist's reframing, however appealing as a longer-term strategy, is not a substitute for answering whether the central claim is true. Peer review's unanimous rejection of "split into three papers" as a first move should be treated as decisive, not merely noted.

Concretely: (1) extend the existing 3-seed harness to the full 7-arm ablation across all four datasets, minimum 5 seeds, with bootstrap CIs on the PR-AUC delta, replacing the noise-floor heuristic outright; (2) treat the catastrophic-cancellation bug as a possible internal-validity threat and check whether it touched training/eval data, not just serving; (3) rerun the k6/load-test claims with repeated trials before presenting them with the same confidence as the (currently unverified) ML results; (4) only after the core numbers are confirmed or killed, decide whether the paper's thesis becomes "topology helps conditionally" (Expansionist/First Principles framing) or whether the systems and reproducibility material is strong enough to justify a split.

## The One Thing to Do First

Before adding a single seed anywhere: **rerun the banksim TGN/ablation pipeline on a second machine with the same fixed seed and compare to the committed 0.938 result.** This is the cheapest possible experiment (one rerun, already-known-to-be-unstable pipeline) and it is the single most diagnostic test available — if the second-machine number lands near 0.845 rather than confirming the +0.097 lift, the entire central thesis is a reproducibility artifact and no amount of additional seeding or CI-wrapping will save it; if it holds, everything else (multi-seed ablation, CIs, reframing) proceeds on solid ground. This gates every other decision in the paper and was caught only in peer review — it should be run first, today, before committing further effort in any direction.
