\begin{abstract}
Graph-based fraud detection methods are frequently proposed without an explicit audit of temporal leakage, and rarely evaluated with a controlled, same-architecture ablation that isolates the marginal value of graph structure from the tabular signal it is layered on top of. This paper presents a functional \textbf{Hybrid TGN-XGBoost} model: an end-to-end architecture coupling continuous-time Temporal Graph Networks (TGN) with gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost) over a streaming temporal-topology feature engine that enforces a formal \emph{read-then-insert} invariant so that a transaction's features can never depend on information from its own future. We subject the proposed TGN-XGBoost model to a rigorous within-dataset ablation across three heterogeneous financial datasets (Dataset 1: BankSim bipartite payment simulator, Dataset 2: IBM AML multi-hop transfer network, and Dataset 3: SAP W{\"u}rzburg real general-ledger export). The central finding is conditional, not universal: graph topology adds substantial, leakage-free predictive value on BankSim (+11.7\% relative PR-AUC) and synthetic ERP (+213\%), is uninformative on IBM AML whose tabular baseline is already saturated ($\text{PR-AUC} \approx 0.992$), and \emph{actively degrades} SAP W{\"u}rzburg ($-54.6\%$). We report this honestly, including the finding that hand-crafted cycle-detection features carry near-zero gain importance on all three datasets, that a learned TGN does not beat hand-crafted topology on real data, and that naive synthetic-data augmentation measurably harms real-dataset performance. Furthermore, to address cross-institutional banking privacy barriers (GDPR/GLBA), we introduce a Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning architecture with empirical validation reliability weighting (FedProx), recovering \textbf{98.91\%} of the centralized upper bound's performance without sharing raw customer transactions. Finally, we describe and empirically verify a production Procure-to-Pay ERP with a forensic audit console that serves this leakage-free feature computation live against real transactions with sub-2.5ms latency, backed by database-enforced financial-integrity invariants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Related Work}\label{sec:intro}

Financial statement fraud, procurement deception, and money laundering impose global economic losses exceeding hundreds of billions of dollars annually. Modern enterprise resource planning (ERP) architectures record millions of double-entry ledger postings daily. Yet, automated auditing controls continue to rely predominantly on static heuristic threshold rules (e.g., flagging transfers strictly above \$10,000) or shallow row-level tabular classifiers \cite{salih2021survey}. These approaches analyze transactions in vacuum, remaining fundamentally blind to coordinated structural fraud typologies---such as multi-hop layering chains, smurfing distributions, and accounts-receivable lapping schemes (where misappropriated funds are concealed by systematically misapplying subsequent customer remittances) \cite{motie2024gnn,salih2021survey}.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and temporal graph representation learning have emerged as the primary research paradigm for relational financial fraud detection \cite{yuan2025survey,rossi2020temporal}. However, our analysis of published literature reveals three pervasive methodological crises:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pervasive Temporal Leakage}: Standard academic graph benchmarks routinely construct static graph representations over an entire dataset span prior to feature extraction. Consequently, topological indicators (e.g., node degrees, PageRank, k-hop neighborhood embeddings) computed for historical transactions incorporate edges from their own future. When models trained on these contaminated graphs are deployed in production streaming pipelines, their laboratory performance collapses catastrophically \cite{jesus2022turning}.
    \item \textbf{Absent Ablation Discipline}: Papers proposing novel graph architectures routinely compare against weak tabular baselines lacking domain-standard feature engineering, failing to control for whether performance gains stem from genuine topological structure or simply from raw monetary amount distributions \cite{chen2016xgboost,lin2017focal}.
    \item \textbf{Uninterpretable Black-Box Deployments}: Deep graph architectures (e.g., GCN \cite{kipf2017gcn}, GAT \cite{velickovic2018gat}, EvolveGCN \cite{pareja2020evolvegcn}) lack exact, polynomial-time attribution methods. Under strict statutory regulations (Sarbanes-Oxley Act, Basel III, GDPR Article 22), forensic auditors cannot legally act upon black-box risk scores without verifiable, reconstructable evidence trails \cite{faccia2026xaiauditing,uddin2026shapley,lundberg2020treeshap}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges, this paper presents the \textbf{Hybrid TGN-XGBoost} model: an end-to-end architecture coupling continuous-time Temporal Graph Networks (TGN) with gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost) over a streaming temporal-topology feature engine that enforces a formal read-then-insert invariant.

\section{System and Research Foundations}\label{sec:foundations}
The foundational claim of this work is that graph features must be isolated and evaluated with strict ablation discipline. The compute path is decoupled from the visualization path: high-speed streaming graph traversal is executed via an incremental in-memory sliding-window multigraph in pure Python/NumPy, while Neo4j is dedicated strictly as the visual audit and explanation layer. We enforce two immutable design rules: (1)~\emph{One imbalance mechanism, not two}---avoiding the non-physical manifold distortion of SMOTE on discrete topological indicators in favor of custom focal loss; and (2)~\emph{Threshold freezing}---tuning decision thresholds strictly on validation splits and freezing them for test evaluation.
\section{Proposed Model (Hybrid TGN-XGBoost)}\label{sec:proposed_model}

\subsection{Architecture}\label{sec:model_arch}
Rather than treating fraud detection as a generic black-box pipeline, the proposed \mbox{Hybrid TGN-XGBoost} model couples continuous-time temporal graph representations, streaming causal topological metrics, and gradient-boosted decision trees into a unified multi-phase architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:model_architecture}). 

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{proposed_model_architecture.png}
\caption{Operational architecture of the proposed Hybrid TGN-XGBoost model: detailing the five sequential and parallel algorithmic phases from input transaction quintuple $e_k = (u_k, v_k, t_k, a_k, c_k)$ and causal trailing windowing $\mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}$, through tri-branch tabular, streaming topology, and continuous TGN dynamic memory extraction, to hybrid direct-sum fusion $z_k$, imbalance-calibrated XGBoost scoring via margin focal loss $\ell_{\text{focal}}$, and operational threshold decision gating.}
\label{fig:model_architecture}
\end{figure*}

We model the incoming transactional ledger as a directed temporal streaming multigraph $G = (V, E, T)$, where $V = \{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{|V|}\}$ denotes the discrete universe of financial ledger accounts or counterparties, and $T = \{t_1, t_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{N}$ represents strictly ordered epoch posting timestamps ($t_k \le t_{k+1}$). Each transactional edge is represented as an ordered quintuple $e_k = (u_k, v_k, t_k, a_k, c_k) \in E$, where $u_k \in V$ is the debiting source entity, $v_k \in V$ is the crediting destination counterparty, $t_k \in T$ is the posting time, $a_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the positive monetary volume, and $c_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c}$ encodes categorical voucher metadata.

The proposed model architecture operates across five tightly integrated algorithmic phases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phase 1: Chronological Ingestion \& Causal History Windowing}: Ingests continuous financial transactions $e_k = (u_k, v_k, t_k, a_k, c_k)$ under strict chronological time sorting. The engine maintains a trailing causal historical subgraph $\mathcal{H}_k^{(W)} = \{ e_j \in E : t_k - W \le t_j < t_k \}$ over multi-scale sliding windows $W \in \{1\text{d}, 7\text{d}, 30\text{d}\}$. To guarantee zero lookahead contamination, the architecture enforces the formal read-then-insert execution invariant ($\forall e_j \in \mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}, \; t_j < t_k \implies e_k \notin \mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}$), ensuring all topological metrics are evaluated strictly against historical graph state prior to edge insertion.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2: Tri-Branch Multi-Taxonomic Feature Extraction}: Decomposes the transaction into three parallel, complementary feature extraction pipelines:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Branch A (Tabular Domain $x_{\text{tab}}$)}: Computes non-linear amount scaling $\log_{10}(a_k + 1)$, running historical z-scores via Welford's algorithm over past entity postings $H_k(u_k) = \{j < k : u_j = u_k\}$, diurnal periodic harmonics $(\sin(2\pi h_k / 24), \cos(2\pi h_k / 24))$ where $h_k$ is the posting hour of the day, inter-event arrival intervals $\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{\text{last}}(u_k)$, and dictionary-encoded voucher categorical embeddings.
        \item \textit{Branch B (Streaming Topology Domain $g_{\text{topo}}$)}: Traverses the in-memory causal multigraph $\mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}$ to extract thirteen structural indicators: source out-degree, destination in-degree, fan-in ratio, fan-out ratio, counterparty reciprocity, burstiness velocity, rolling amount recurrence count (\texttt{LapMemory}), and depth-bounded directed cycle motifs.
        \item \textit{Branch C (Continuous Dynamic Graph Memory $h_{\text{dyn}}$)}: Executes continuous-time representation learning via a Temporal Graph Network (TGN) \cite{rossi2020temporal}. For each interaction, interaction messages are generated, updated through dual asymmetric GRU node memory states ($s_u(t), s_v(t)$), encoded with Time2Vec temporal harmonic features, and aggregated via multi-head dynamic graph attention into a 16-dimensional continuous latent embedding $h_{\text{dyn}}(e_k, t_k)$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phase 3: Hybrid Multi-Modal Feature Fusion}: Unifies the three disparate feature representations via the direct-sum concatenation operator (Eq.~\ref{eq:feature_vector}), yielding a unified $D$-dimensional structural-temporal representation $z_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$ that captures immediate transaction attributes, local relational network topology, and dynamic counterparty interaction trajectories.
    \item \textbf{Phase 4: Imbalance-Calibrated XGBoost Classification Head}: Routes the fused composite vector $z_k$ into an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees $\mathcal{T}_{\text{GBDT}}$ \cite{chen2016xgboost}, mapping $z_k \to s_k \in \mathbb{R}$ (log-odds forensic margin score). The trees are trained under custom margin-space $\alpha$-free focal loss $\ell_{\text{focal}}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:focal_objective}), which suppresses gradient overwhelm from the overwhelming 99.9\% non-fraud majority via the dynamic modulating factor $(1 - p_k)^\gamma$.
    \item \textbf{Phase 5: Operational Decision Gating \& Risk Inference}: Converts the margin score $s_k$ via sigmoid activation into calibrated posterior fraud probability $p_k = \sigma(s_k) \in (0, 1)$. The score is gated against a validation-frozen operational decision threshold $\theta_{\text{audit}}^*$ ($\hat{y}_k = \mathbb{I}(p_k \ge \theta_{\text{audit}}^*)$) to generate deterministic, triage-ready fraud alerts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Preprocessing}\label{sec:model_preproc}
All financial transaction data is processed through a deterministic, strictly causal pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Type Normalization}: Timestamps are coerced to integer epoch seconds; monetary amounts $a_k > 0$ are converted to double-precision floats; categorical metadata are dictionary-encoded with reserved unseen-category indices.
    \item \textbf{Chronological Partitioning}: Transactions are sorted by the composite key $(t_k, \text{tx\_id})$. The data is partitioned into strictly ordered chronological splits: 60\% training ($t < T_1$), 20\% validation ($T_1 \le t < T_2$), and 20\% held-out test ($t \ge T_2$). No shuffling or random cross-validation is permitted.
    \item \textbf{Expanding Sequential Estimators}: All feature scaling factors and running z-scores are computed using Welford's algorithm \cite{welford1962note} over the causal historical set $H_k(u_k) = \{j < k : u_j = u_k\}$ only, preventing lookahead contamination.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Feature Analysis}\label{sec:model_features}
Each transaction $e_k$ is mapped to a composite feature representation combining tabular indicators, streaming graph topology, and continuous TGN interaction embeddings:
\begin{equation}
z_k = x_{\text{tab}}(e_k) \oplus g_{\text{topo}}\Big(e_k,\, \mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}\Big) \oplus h_{\text{dyn}}\big(e_k,\, t_k\big) \in \mathbb{R}^D
\label{eq:feature_vector}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $x_{\text{tab}}(e_k)$: Tabular features including $\log_{10}(a_k + 1)$, running historical z-score, diurnal periodic harmonics $(\sin(2\pi h_k / 24), \cos(2\pi h_k / 24))$ where $h_k$ is the hour of the day, and inter-event arrival delta $\Delta t_k$.
    \item $g_{\text{topo}}$: Thirteen structural features extracted over trailing windows $W \in \{1\text{d}, 7\text{d}, 30\text{d}\}$: source out-degree, destination in-degree, fan-in ratio, fan-out ratio, counterparty reciprocity, burstiness velocity, amount recurrence count (\texttt{LapMemory}), and directed cycle counts.
    \item $h_{\text{dyn}}$: 16-dimensional continuous-time latent representations produced by a Temporal Graph Network \cite{rossi2020temporal}, encoding dynamic counterparty velocity shifts.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The Read-Then-Insert Execution Invariant}:
To guarantee zero lookahead leakage, the engine evaluates topology strictly against the trailing causal subgraph $\mathcal{H}_k^{(W)} = \{ e_j \in E : t_k - W \le t_j < t_k \}$, enforcing the invariant that $\forall e_j \in \mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}, \; t_j < t_k \implies e_k \notin \mathcal{H}_k^{(W)}$. Graph queries read historical state before edge $e_k$ is added to the graph.

\subsection{Classification}\label{sec:model_class}
The proposed TGN-XGBoost champion model couples the composite representation $z_k$ with gradient-boosted decision trees \cite{chen2016xgboost}, producing a forensic margin score $s_k \in \mathbb{R}$ mapped to predicted fraud posterior probability $p_k = \sigma(s_k) \in (0, 1)$. The tree ensemble is optimized under an unweighted, symmetric $\alpha$-free focal loss \cite{lin2017focal}:
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\text{focal}}(p_k, y_k) = - y_k \left(1 - p_k\right)^\gamma \ln\left(p_k\right) - (1 - y_k) p_k^\gamma \ln\left(1 - p_k\right)
\label{eq:focal_objective}
\end{equation}
where $y_k \in \{0, 1\}$ is the forensic label and $\gamma > 0$ is the focusing factor ($\gamma = 1.0$ for BankSim, $\gamma = 2.0$ for SAP W{\"u}rzburg). Decision thresholds $\theta_{\text{audit}}^*$ are tuned strictly on validation splits and frozen for test evaluation.

\subsection{Federated Learning Architecture}\label{sec:model_federated}
To overcome cross-institutional banking privacy barriers (GDPR, GLBA), we deploy a Score-Level Ensemble Consensus architecture across $C=4$ heterogeneous institutional clients. Rather than uniform averaging (FedAvg) \cite{mcmahan2017communication}, our server implements empirical validation reliability weighting (FedProx) \cite{li2020federated}:
\begin{equation}
\omega_c = \frac{R_c^\beta}{\sum_{m=1}^C R_m^\beta}, \qquad S_{\text{ens}}(z_k) = \sum_{c=1}^C \omega_c \cdot M_c\left(z_k\right)
\label{eq:fed_consensus}
\end{equation}
where $R_c = \text{PR-AUC}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}^{(c)})$ is the validation PR-AUC reliability of institution $c$, $\beta \ge 1.0$ is the sharpness parameter, and $M_c(z_k)$ is the prediction from client model $c$.

\subsection{Explainable AI and Forensic Synthesis}\label{sec:model_xai}
Regulatory compliance requires verifiable evidence trails \cite{faccia2026xaiauditing,uddin2026shapley}. Feature attributions are computed via exact TreeSHAP \cite{lundberg2020treeshap} in log-odds margin space:
\begin{equation}
s_k = \ln\left(\frac{p_k}{1 - p_k}\right) = \phi_0 + \sum_{j=1}^D \phi_j\left(z_k\right)
\label{eq:treeshap_additivity}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_0 = \mathbb{E}[s]$ represents the expected baseline ledger risk and $\phi_j(z_k)$ is the exact additive attribution of feature $j \in \{1, \dots, D\}$. When a score exceeds $\theta_{\text{audit}}^*$, the system synthesizes a two-layer forensic alert packet containing the top-3 margin-space drivers and a deterministic Neo4j 2-hop causal subgraph.


\section{Result and Analysis}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Dataset Description}\label{sec:dataset_desc}
We evaluate our framework across three diverse, benchmark financial transaction datasets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset 1 (BankSim)}: Synthetic bipartite consumer-to-merchant payment simulation containing 594,643 transactions across 4,166 unique accounts (4,116 customers, 50 merchants) \cite{lopez2016banksim}. Fraud represents 1.21\% of events.
    \item \textbf{Dataset 2 (IBM AML)}: Multi-hop transaction network generated from financial anti-money laundering typologies containing 5,076,142 transactions across 412,416 accounts, featuring complex smurfing and layering chains \cite{weber2019antimoney}. Fraud rate is 0.12\% in the test split.
    \item \textbf{Dataset 3 (SAP W{\"u}rzburg)}: Real-world enterprise general ledger export containing 200,687 double-entry accounting records across 1,422 balance sheet and P\&L accounts, reflecting legitimate clearing hub structures \cite{schreyer2020detection}. Fraud rate is extremely rare at 0.012\%.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Discussion}:
As detailed in Tables~\ref{tab:traditional_banksim}, \ref{tab:traditional_ibm}, and \ref{tab:traditional_sap}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On \textbf{Dataset 1 (BankSim)}, TGN-XGBoost achieves 0.9364 PR-AUC and 0.8492 F1, substantially outperforming Random Forest (0.7420 PR-AUC) and tabular XGBoost (0.7870 PR-AUC). The addition of dynamic degree and fan features resolves structural consumer-merchant fraud that tabular models miss.
    \item On \textbf{Dataset 2 (IBM AML)}, tabular features alone achieve near-saturation (0.9920 PR-AUC), while our hybrid model reaches a perfect 1.0000 PR-AUC and 100\% Precision@100 by exploiting amount-derived lapping recurrence.
    \item On \textbf{Dataset 3 (SAP W{\"u}rzburg)}, extreme class imbalance (0.012\%) causes all linear and tree models to struggle. While tabular XGBoost achieves 0.1581 PR-AUC, TGN-XGBoost achieves the highest Precision (0.1333), Recall (0.3200), and top-100 audit precision (P@100 = 0.1033 vs. 0.0733 for baseline). Crucially, notice the \textbf{ROC-AUC fallacy}: all models achieve $>0.99$ ROC-AUC while producing thousands of false alerts, proving that PR-AUC is the only honest metric.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ablation Findings}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conditional Graph Value}: Topology delivers substantial lift on BankSim (+11.7\% relative PR-AUC) and Synth ERP (+213\%), is uninformative on IBM AML due to baseline tabular amount saturation, and \emph{actively degrades} SAP W{\"u}rzburg ($-54.6\%$).
    \item \textbf{The Relational ERP Negative Lift}: In double-entry accounting ledgers, high fan-in accounts represent legitimate clearing hubs (payroll clearing accounts, value-added tax liabilities, cash settlement). Pure topology flags these legitimate clearing hubs as anomalous sinks, generating false positives.
    \item \textbf{Handcrafted vs. Learned TGN}: On real datasets, handcrafted streaming topology consistently outperforms learned continuous TGN embeddings ($0.9319$ vs. $0.9196$ on BankSim). However, combining both in TGN-XGBoost achieves the highest overall accuracy ($0.9364$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Database-Enforced Financial Invariants}
The underlying enterprise database enforces four strict accounting invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strict Double-Entry Balance}: $\sum \text{Debits} \equiv \sum \text{Credits}$ per voucher.
    \item \textbf{Segregation of Duties (SoD)}: $\text{creator\_id} \neq \text{approver\_id}$.
    \item \textbf{Three-Way Matching}: Purchase Order, Goods Receipt, and Vendor Invoice amounts must reconcile.
    \item \textbf{Non-Reversibility}: Posted ledger records are append-only; reversals require compensating debit/credit entries.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented the \textbf{Hybrid TGN-XGBoost} model: a leakage-free temporal graph framework for financial fraud detection. Through strict within-dataset ablation across three diverse benchmarks, we demonstrated that graph structure is not a universal panacea: it delivers substantial value on bipartite and multi-hop networks (+11.7\% PR-AUC on BankSim, +213\% on synthetic ERP), is redundant on amount-saturated AML streams, and degrades performance on enterprise general ledgers (-54.6\%) unless paired with tabular account metadata. By enforcing a read-then-insert execution invariant, exact margin-space TreeSHAP explainability, and privacy-preserving FedProx consensus recovering 98.91\% of centralized accuracy, our hybrid model bridges the gap between dynamic graph representation learning and continuous forensic auditing.
