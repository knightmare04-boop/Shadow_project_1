\begin{abstract}
Graph-based fraud detection methods are frequently proposed without an explicit audit of temporal leakage, and rarely evaluated with a controlled, same-architecture ablation that isolates the marginal value of graph structure from the tabular signal it is layered on top of. This paper presents \textbf{ChronoSentry} (\textbf{Chrono}logically-Hardened \textbf{Sentry} for Streaming Ledger Forensics): an end-to-end framework featuring a leakage-free streaming temporal-topology feature engine that enforces a formal \emph{read-then-insert} invariant so that a transaction's features can never depend on information from its own future. We subject ChronoSentry to a rigorous within-dataset ablation across three heterogeneous financial datasets (Dataset 1: BankSim bipartite payment simulator, Dataset 2: IBM AML multi-hop transfer network, and Dataset 3: SAP W{\"u}rzburg real general-ledger export). The central finding is conditional, not universal: graph topology adds substantial, leakage-free predictive value on BankSim (+11.7\% relative PR-AUC) and synthetic ERP (+213\%), is uninformative on IBM AML whose tabular baseline is already saturated ($\text{PR-AUC} \approx 0.992$), and \emph{actively degrades} SAP W{\"u}rzburg ($-54.6\%$). We report this honestly, including the finding that hand-crafted cycle-detection features carry near-zero gain importance on all three datasets, that a learned temporal graph network (TGN) does not beat hand-crafted topology on real data, and that naive synthetic-data augmentation measurably harms real-dataset performance. Furthermore, to address cross-institutional banking privacy barriers (GDPR/GLBA), ChronoSentry introduces a Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning architecture with empirical validation reliability weighting (FedProx), recovering \textbf{98.91\%} of the centralized upper bound's performance without sharing raw customer transactions. Finally, we describe and empirically verify a production Procure-to-Pay ERP with a forensic audit console that serves this leakage-free feature computation live against real transactions with sub-2.5ms latency, backed by database-enforced financial-integrity invariants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Related Work}\label{sec:intro}

Financial statement fraud, procurement deception, and money laundering impose global economic losses exceeding hundreds of billions of dollars annually. Modern enterprise resource planning (ERP) architectures record millions of double-entry ledger postings daily. Yet, automated auditing controls continue to rely predominantly on static heuristic threshold rules (e.g., flagging transfers strictly above \$10,000) or shallow row-level tabular classifiers \cite{salih2021survey}. These approaches analyze transactions in vacuum, remaining fundamentally blind to coordinated structural fraud typologies---such as multi-hop layering chains, smurfing distributions, and accounts-receivable lapping schemes (where misappropriated funds are concealed by systematically misapplying subsequent customer remittances) \cite{motie2024gnn,salih2021survey}.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and temporal graph representation learning have emerged as the primary research paradigm for relational financial fraud detection \cite{yuan2025survey,rossi2020temporal}. However, our analysis of published literature reveals three pervasive methodological crises:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pervasive Temporal Leakage}: Standard academic graph benchmarks routinely construct static graph representations over an entire dataset span prior to feature extraction. Consequently, topological indicators (e.g., node degrees, PageRank, k-hop neighborhood embeddings) computed for historical transactions incorporate edges from their own future. When models trained on these contaminated graphs are deployed in production streaming pipelines, their laboratory performance collapses catastrophically \cite{jesus2022turning}.
    \item \textbf{Absent Ablation Discipline}: Papers proposing novel graph architectures routinely compare against weak tabular baselines lacking domain-standard feature engineering, failing to control for whether performance gains stem from genuine topological structure or simply from raw monetary amount distributions \cite{chen2016xgboost,lin2017focal}.
    \item \textbf{Uninterpretable Black-Box Deployments}: Deep graph architectures (e.g., GCN \cite{kipf2017gcn}, GAT \cite{velickovic2018gat}, EvolveGCN \cite{pareja2020evolvegcn}) lack exact, polynomial-time attribution methods. Under strict statutory regulations (Sarbanes-Oxley Act, Basel III, GDPR Article 22), forensic auditors cannot legally act upon black-box risk scores without verifiable, reconstructable evidence trails \cite{faccia2026xaiauditing,uddin2026shapley,lundberg2020treeshap}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{ChronoSentry}: a comprehensive framework combining a streaming, leakage-free temporal topology engine, exact tree-native margin explainability, privacy-preserving federated score consensus, and an enterprise-grade continuous auditing console.

\section{System and Research Foundations}\label{sec:foundations}
The foundational claim of this work is that graph features must be isolated and evaluated with strict ablation discipline. The compute path is decoupled from the visualization path: high-speed streaming graph traversal is executed via an incremental in-memory sliding-window multigraph in pure Python/NumPy, while Neo4j is dedicated strictly as the visual audit and explanation layer. We enforce two immutable design rules: (1)~\emph{One imbalance mechanism, not two}---avoiding the non-physical manifold distortion of SMOTE on discrete topological indicators in favor of custom focal loss; and (2)~\emph{Threshold freezing}---tuning decision thresholds strictly on validation splits and freezing them for test evaluation.

\subsection{Architecture}\label{sec:model_arch}
The proposed ChronoSentry architecture bridges dynamic transactional multigraphs, continuous temporal graph representations, gradient-boosted decision trees, and regulatory forensic auditing (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline_overview}). 

We model the incoming transactional ledger as a directed temporal streaming multigraph:
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak{G} = \left(\mathcal{A},\, \mathcal{X},\, \mathbb{T}\right)
\label{eq:multigraph}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal{A} = \{\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_{|\mathcal{A}|}\}$ denotes the discrete universe of financial ledger accounts, counterparties, or general ledger entities.
    \item $\mathbb{T} = \{\tau_1, \tau_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{N}$ represents strictly ordered epoch posting timestamps ($\tau_k \le \tau_{k+1}$).
    \item Each transactional edge is represented as an ordered quintuple:
    \begin{equation}
    \xi_k = \left(\mathfrak{s}_k,\, \mathfrak{d}_k,\, \tau_k,\, \nu_k,\, \mathbf{c}_k\right) \in \mathcal{X}
    \label{eq:edge_quintuple}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathfrak{s}_k \in \mathcal{A}$ is the debiting/source entity, $\mathfrak{d}_k \in \mathcal{A}$ is the crediting/destination counterparty, $\tau_k \in \mathbb{T}$ is the posting time, $\nu_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the positive monetary volume, and $\mathbf{c}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c}$ encodes ledger categorical attributes (posting key, document voucher type).
\end{itemize}
\emph{Distinction from Prior Literature}: Unlike Rossi et al.~\cite{rossi2020temporal} who define dynamic interaction graphs using generic node indices $(u, v)$ and continuous time vectors $e_{uv}(t)$, our formulation explicitly anchors monetary flow $\nu_k$ and accounting metadata $\mathbf{c}_k$ over discrete ledger entity spaces $\mathcal{A}$.

ChronoSentry comprises six tightly coupled operational modules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Chronological Ingestion \& Graph Projection}: Sequentially maps continuous transactions to dynamic graph edges under strict time sorting.
    \item \textbf{Streaming Leakage-Free Topology Engine}: Evaluates sliding-window structural metrics solely against historical graph state $\mathfrak{G}_{< \tau_k}$.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Dynamic Graph Representation}: Maintains continuous-time node memory states via a Temporal Graph Network (TGN).
    \item \textbf{Imbalance-Calibrated Ensemble Classifier}: Scores transactions via an XGBoost gradient-boosted decision tree head optimized with an $\alpha$-free focal loss.
    \item \textbf{Privacy-Preserving Federated Consensus}: Aggregates decentralized bank models via validation reliability weighting (FedProx).
    \item \textbf{Forensic Explainability \& Alert Synthesis}: Computes exact margin-space TreeSHAP feature attributions and extracts 2-hop causal subgraphs via Neo4j Cypher queries.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Preprocessing}\label{sec:model_preproc}
All financial transaction data is processed through a deterministic, strictly causal pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Type Normalization}: Timestamps are coerced to integer epoch seconds; monetary amounts $\nu_k > 0$ are converted to double-precision floats; categorical metadata (payment channels, transaction types) are dictionary-encoded with reserved unseen-category indices.
    \item \textbf{Chronological Partitioning}: Transactions are sorted by the composite key $(\tau_k, \text{tx\_id})$. The data is partitioned into strictly ordered chronological splits: 60\% training ($\tau < T_1$), 20\% validation ($T_1 \le \tau < T_2$), and 20\% held-out test ($\tau \ge T_2$). No shuffling, random cross-validation, or bidirectional temporal imputation is permitted.
    \item \textbf{Expanding Sequential Estimators}: All feature scaling factors and running z-scores are computed using an entity-conditioned expanding estimator \cite{welford1962note} over the causal historical set $\mathbb{H}_k(\mathfrak{s}_k) = \{j < k : \mathfrak{s}_j = \mathfrak{s}_k\}$ with cardinality $n_k = |\mathbb{H}_k(\mathfrak{s}_k)| + 1$:
    \begin{align}
        \bar{\nu}_k &= \bar{\nu}_{k-1} + \frac{\nu_k - \bar{\nu}_{k-1}}{n_k} \label{eq:welford_mean} \\
        \mathcal{Q}_k &= \mathcal{Q}_{k-1} + (\nu_k - \bar{\nu}_{k-1})(\nu_k - \bar{\nu}_k) \label{eq:welford_m2} \\
        \zeta_k &= \frac{\nu_k - \bar{\nu}_k}{\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{Q}_k}{\max(1, n_k - 1)} + \varepsilon_{\text{reg}}}} \label{eq:welford_z}
    \end{align}
    where $\zeta_k$ is the running historical z-score and $\varepsilon_{\text{reg}} = 10^{-6}$ guarantees numerical stability under zero-variance initialization. No whole-dataset normalization or future statistics ever contaminate historical feature rows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Feature Analysis}\label{sec:model_features}
Each transaction $\xi_k$ is mapped to a combined feature vector:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\Phi}(\xi_k) = \mathbf{\psi}_{\text{tab}}(\xi_k) \oplus \mathbf{\Omega}_{\text{topo}}\Big(\xi_k,\, \mathfrak{G}_{< \tau_k}^{(W)}\Big) \oplus \mathbf{h}_{\text{dyn}}\big(\xi_k,\, \tau_k\big) \in \mathbb{R}^D
\label{eq:feature_vector}
\end{equation}
across three distinct taxonomic families:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ordinary Tabular Features ($\mathbf{\psi}_{\text{tab}}(\xi_k)$)}: $\log_{10}(\nu_k + 1)$, running causal z-scores $\zeta_k$, diurnal periodic encodings $(\sin(2\pi \hbar_k / 24), \cos(2\pi \hbar_k / 24))$ where $\hbar_k$ is posting hour, and inter-event arrival deltas $\Delta \tau_k = \tau_k - \tau_{\text{last}}(\mathfrak{s}_k)$.
    \item \textbf{Handcrafted Streaming Topology ($\mathbf{\Omega}_{\text{topo}}$)}: Thirteen structural and behavioral features extracted dynamically by the streaming engine over trailing windows $W \in \{1\text{d}, 7\text{d}, 30\text{d}\}$: source out-degree, destination in-degree, fan-in ratio, fan-out ratio, counterparty reciprocity, burstiness velocity ($\text{deg}_{1d}/\text{deg}_{7d}$), amount recurrence count (\texttt{LapMemory}), and directed length-3 and length-4 cycle counts.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Dynamic Graph Embeddings ($\mathbf{h}_{\text{dyn}}$)}: 16-dimensional continuous-time latent representations produced by a Temporal Graph Network (TGN) \cite{rossi2020temporal}, encoding sequential memory states and interaction dynamics between counterparties.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The Read-Then-Insert Execution Invariant}:
To guarantee zero lookahead leakage, the engine enforces a strict structural invariant over the trailing causal subgraph:
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak{G}_{< \tau_k}^{(W)} = \left\{ \xi_j \in \mathcal{X} : \tau_k - W \le \tau_j < \tau_k \right\}
\label{eq:causal_window}
\end{equation}
which satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\forall \xi_j \in \mathfrak{G}_{< \tau_k}^{(W)}, \quad \tau_j < \tau_k \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \xi_k \notin \mathfrak{G}_{< \tau_k}^{(W)}
\label{eq:leakage_invariant}
\end{equation}
Operationally:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Read}: Query \texttt{WindowGraph} for transaction $k$'s fan-in, fan-out, destination in-degree, source out-degree, and (if $\mathfrak{s}_k \neq \mathfrak{d}_k$) run bounded cycle search; query \texttt{LapMemory} for amount recurrence against historical records.
    \item \textbf{Insert}: Only after all reads return does the engine call \texttt{WindowGraph.add($\tau_k, \mathfrak{s}_k, \mathfrak{d}_k, \nu_k$)} and \texttt{LapMemory.record(...)} for transaction $k$ itself.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Dynamic Interaction State Formulation}:
Unlike Rossi et al.~\cite{rossi2020temporal} who formulate node states using generic $s_i(t)$ and $m_i(t)$, ChronoSentry maintains an entity state $\mathbf{\eta}_\alpha(\tau) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{mem}}}$. Upon the arrival of edge $\xi_k$, the interaction transition message $\mathbf{\rho}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k)$ is synthesized via:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\rho}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k) = \mathcal{M}_{\text{enc}}\Big(\mathbf{\eta}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k^-) \parallel \mathbf{\eta}_{\mathfrak{d}_k}(\tau_k^-) \parallel \log_{10}(\nu_k + 1) \parallel \mathbf{\phi}_{\mathbb{T}}(\Delta \tau_k)\Big)
\label{eq:tgn_msg}
\end{equation}
where $\parallel$ denotes tensor concatenation, $\tau_k^-$ denotes the instant immediately preceding $\tau_k$, and $\mathbf{\phi}_{\mathbb{T}}(\cdot)$ is a Bochner cosine-frequency time encoding:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\phi}_{\mathbb{T}}(\Delta \tau_k) = \left[ \cos\left(\omega_1 \Delta \tau_k + \beta_1\right),\, \dots,\, \cos\left(\omega_K \Delta \tau_k + \beta_K\right) \right]^\top
\label{eq:time_kernel}
\end{equation}
The entity memory is updated via a Gated Recurrent Unit transition:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{\eta}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k) = \operatorname{GRU}\Big(\mathbf{\rho}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k),\, \mathbf{\eta}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k^-)\Big)
\label{eq:tgn_gru}
\end{equation}
The pairwise dynamic embedding vector is projected through a parameterized LeakyReLU layer ($\alpha = 0.1$):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{dyn}}(\xi_k, \tau_k) = \max\Big(0.1 \cdot \mathbf{\Gamma}_k,\, \mathbf{\Gamma}_k\Big), \quad \mathbf{\Gamma}_k = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}\left[\mathbf{\eta}_{\mathfrak{s}_k}(\tau_k) \parallel \mathbf{\eta}_{\mathfrak{d}_k}(\tau_k)\right] + \mathbf{b}_{\text{proj}}
\label{eq:tgn_proj}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Classification}\label{sec:model_class}
The finalized ChronoSentry champion model is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{ChronoSentry}}(\xi_k) = \mathcal{T}_{\text{GBDT}}\Big(\mathbf{\Phi}(\xi_k);\, \mathcal{J}_{\text{focal}},\, \Theta^*\Big)
\label{eq:champion_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_{\text{GBDT}}$ denotes an ensemble of gradient-boosted decision trees \cite{chen2016xgboost} optimized under an unweighted, symmetric $\alpha$-free focal loss \cite{lin2017focal}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}_{\text{focal}}(\hat{\pi}_k, y_k) = - y_k \left(1 - \hat{\pi}_k\right)^\gamma \ln\left(\hat{\pi}_k\right) - (1 - y_k) \hat{\pi}_k^\gamma \ln\left(1 - \hat{\pi}_k\right)
\label{eq:focal_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\pi}_k = \sigma(\theta_k) = (1 + e^{-\theta_k})^{-1}$ is the sigmoid posterior of predicted log-odds margin $\theta_k \in \mathbb{R}$, $y_k \in \{0, 1\}$ is the ground-truth forensic label, and $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the focusing factor ($\gamma = 1.0$ for BankSim, $\gamma = 2.0$ for SAP W{\"u}rzburg).

The closed-form directional first-order gradient $g_k = \frac{\partial \mathcal{J}_{\text{focal}}}{\partial \theta_k}$ is:
\begin{equation}
g_k = \begin{cases}
\gamma \hat{\pi}_k \left(1 - \hat{\pi}_k\right)^\gamma \ln\left(\hat{\pi}_k\right) - \left(1 - \hat{\pi}_k\right)^{\gamma + 1}, & \text{if } y_k = 1 \\
\hat{\pi}_k^{\gamma + 1} - \gamma \left(1 - \hat{\pi}_k\right) \hat{\pi}_k^\gamma \ln\left(1 - \hat{\pi}_k\right), & \text{if } y_k = 0
\end{cases}
\label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
The second-order curvature hessian $H_k = \frac{\partial^2 \mathcal{J}_{\text{focal}}}{\partial \theta_k^2}$ is evaluated via central difference perturbation with step $\delta_\epsilon = 10^{-4}$:
\begin{equation}
H_k \approx \frac{g_k\left(\theta_k + \delta_\epsilon\right) - g_k\left(\theta_k - \delta_\epsilon\right)}{2\delta_\epsilon}
\label{eq:hessian}
\end{equation}
The operational decision threshold is tuned strictly on validation set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$ and frozen:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\text{audit}}^* = \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta_{\text{sweep}}} \mathcal{F}_1\Big(\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}},\, \theta\Big), \quad \hat{y}_k = \mathbb{I}\left(\hat{\pi}_k \ge \theta_{\text{audit}}^*\right)
\label{eq:threshold_freezing}
\end{equation}
and frozen for test evaluation. Test metrics are never evaluated at an uncalibrated default 0.5 threshold.

\subsection{Federated Learning Architecture}\label{sec:model_federated}
To overcome cross-institutional banking secrecy barriers (GDPR, GLBA), we deploy a Score-Level Ensemble Consensus architecture across $C=4$ heterogeneous institutional clients partitioned via k-means clustering on transaction volume. 

Rather than naive uniform averaging (FedAvg) \cite{mcmahan2017communication}, which allows fraud-starved institutions to dilute the consensus, our server implements empirical validation reliability weighting (FedProx) \cite{li2020federated}. Across $C$ decentralized clients $c \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ with private validation precision-recall area $\mathcal{P}_{\text{val}}^{(c)} = \text{PR-AUC}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}^{(c)})$, the central aggregator computes institutional reliability coefficients:
\begin{equation}
\varpi_c = \frac{\left(\mathcal{P}_{\text{val}}^{(c)}\right)^\kappa}{\sum_{m=1}^C \left(\mathcal{P}_{\text{val}}^{(m)}\right)^\kappa}
\label{eq:fed_weights}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa \ge 1.0$ is the empirical confidence sharpening exponent. The global federated consensus prediction is:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\Pi}_{\text{consensus}}(\xi_k) = \sum_{c=1}^C \varpi_c \cdot \mathcal{M}^{(c)}\left(\mathbf{\Phi}(\xi_k)\right)
\label{eq:fed_consensus}
\end{equation}
Each client trains autonomously on its private transaction partition, early-stops on local validation data, and communicates only prediction scores and validation confidence metrics to the central aggregator.

\subsection{Explainable AI and Forensic Synthesis}\label{sec:model_xai}
Regulatory compliance (Sarbanes-Oxley Act, Basel III) requires verifiable evidence trails \cite{faccia2026xaiauditing,uddin2026shapley}. Every reported feature attribution is computed via TreeSHAP \cite{lundberg2020treeshap} in unconstrained log-odds margin space $\theta_k = \ln\left(\frac{\hat{\pi}_k}{1 - \hat{\pi}_k}\right)$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_k = \Lambda_0 + \sum_{d=1}^D \Lambda_d\Big(\mathbf{\Phi}(\xi_k)\Big)
\label{eq:treeshap_additivity}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda_0 = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}[\theta]$ represents the base ledger log-odds prior, and $\Lambda_d \in \mathbb{R}$ represents the exact, sign-consistent marginal attribution of the $d$-th feature. This executes the exact TreeSHAP recursion as a pure traversal of the fitted tree ensemble, returning signed attributions additive in log-odds space. It is exact (not sampled) and valid under focal gradients.

When an anomaly score exceeds $\theta_{\text{audit}}^*$, the continuous auditing console synthesizes a two-layer forensic alert packet:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Layer 1 (Model Attribution)}: Top 3 signed driving features in log-odds space (e.g., $+0.43\,\text{log\_amount}$, $+0.29\,\text{dest\_in\_degree}$).
    \item \textbf{Layer 2 (Reconstructed Graph Evidence)}: A deterministic Cypher query executing against Neo4j that extracts the historical subgraph surrounding the flagged transaction, confirming counterparty identities and payment paths for forensic review.
\end{itemize}

\section{Result and Analysis}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Dataset Description}\label{sec:dataset_desc}
We evaluate our framework across three diverse, benchmark financial transaction datasets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset 1 (BankSim)}: Synthetic bipartite consumer-to-merchant payment simulation containing 594,643 transactions across 4,166 unique accounts (4,116 customers, 50 merchants) \cite{lopez2016banksim}. Fraud represents 1.21\% of events.
    \item \textbf{Dataset 2 (IBM AML)}: Multi-hop transaction network generated from financial anti-money laundering typologies containing 5,076,142 transactions across 412,416 accounts, featuring complex smurfing and layering chains \cite{weber2019antimoney}. Fraud rate is 0.12\% in the test split.
    \item \textbf{Dataset 3 (SAP W{\"u}rzburg)}: Real-world enterprise general ledger export containing 200,687 double-entry accounting records across 1,422 balance sheet and P\&L accounts, reflecting legitimate clearing hub structures \cite{schreyer2020detection}. Fraud rate is extremely rare at 0.012\%.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Database-Enforced Financial Invariants}
The underlying enterprise database enforces four strict accounting invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strict Double-Entry Balance}: $\sum \text{Debits} \equiv \sum \text{Credits}$ per voucher.
    \item \textbf{Segregation of Duties (SoD)}: $\text{creator\_id} \neq \text{approver\_id}$.
    \item \textbf{Three-Way Matching}: Purchase Order, Goods Receipt, and Vendor Invoice amounts must reconcile.
    \item \textbf{Non-Reversibility}: Posted ledger records are append-only; reversals require compensating debit/credit entries.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented \textbf{ChronoSentry}: a leakage-free temporal graph topology framework for financial fraud detection. Through strict within-dataset ablation across three diverse benchmarks, we demonstrated that graph structure is not a universal panacea: it delivers substantial value on bipartite and multi-hop networks (+11.7\% PR-AUC on BankSim, +213\% on synthetic ERP), is redundant on amount-saturated AML streams, and degrades performance on enterprise general ledgers (-54.6\%) unless paired with tabular account metadata. By enforcing a read-then-insert execution invariant, exact margin-space TreeSHAP explainability, and privacy-preserving FedProx consensus recovering 98.91\% of centralized accuracy, ChronoSentry bridges the gap between academic graph learning and continuous forensic auditing.
