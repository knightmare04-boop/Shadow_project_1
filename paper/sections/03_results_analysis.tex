\section{Result and Analysis}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Dataset Description}\label{sec:dataset_desc}
We evaluate our framework across three diverse, benchmark financial transaction datasets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset 1 (BankSim)}: Synthetic bipartite consumer-to-merchant payment simulation containing 594,643 transactions across 4,166 unique accounts (4,116 customers, 50 merchants) \cite{lopez2016banksim}. Fraud represents 1.21\% of events.
    \item \textbf{Dataset 2 (IBM AML)}: Multi-hop transaction network generated from financial anti-money laundering typologies containing 5,076,142 transactions across 412,416 accounts, featuring complex smurfing and layering chains \cite{weber2019antimoney}. Fraud rate is 0.12\% in the test split.
    \item \textbf{Dataset 3 (SAP W\"urzburg)}: Real-world enterprise general ledger export containing 200,687 double-entry accounting records across 1,422 balance sheet and P\&L accounts, reflecting legitimate clearing hub structures \cite{schreyer2020detection}. Fraud rate is extremely rare at 0.012\%.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Discussion}:
As detailed in Tables~\ref{tab:traditional_banksim}, \ref{tab:traditional_ibm}, and \ref{tab:traditional_sap}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On \textbf{Dataset 1 (BankSim)}, TGN-XGBoost achieves 0.9364 PR-AUC and 0.8492 F1, substantially outperforming Random Forest (0.7420 PR-AUC) and tabular XGBoost (0.7870 PR-AUC). Crucially, notice the \textbf{ROC-AUC fallacy}: all models achieve $>0.99$ ROC-AUC while producing thousands of false alerts, proving that PR-AUC is the only honest metric.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ablation Findings}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conditional Graph Value}: Topology delivers substantial lift on BankSim (+11.7\% relative PR-AUC) and Synth ERP (+213\%), is uninformative on IBM AML due to baseline tabular amount saturation, and \emph{actively degrades} SAP W\"urzburg ($-54.6\%$).
    \item \textbf{The Relational ERP Negative Lift}: In double-entry accounting ledgers, high fan-in accounts represent legitimate clearing hubs (payroll clearing accounts, value-added tax liabilities, cash settlement). Pure topology flags these legitimate clearing hubs as anomalous sinks, generating false positives.
    \item \textbf{Handcrafted vs. Learned TGN}: On real datasets, handcrafted streaming topology consistently outperforms learned continuous TGN embeddings ($0.9319$ vs. $0.9196$ on BankSim). However, combining both in TGN-XGBoost achieves the highest overall accuracy ($0.9364$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Dataset 1 (BankSim) Attribution}
In BankSim, predicted fraud risk is driven by short-term degree bursts and amount scale:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item `dest_in_degree` (+0.5199 gain share): Sudden concentration of payments into a merchant.
    \item `fan_in` (+0.0908 gain share): High number of distinct customers paying into the same counterparty.
    \item `log_amount` (+0.43 mean $|\text{SHAP}|$): Elevated monetary value.
    \item `tgn_s2` / `tgn_d14` (+0.08 mean $|\text{SHAP}|$): Dynamic interaction memory.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Dataset 2 (IBM AML) Attribution}
In IBM AML, amount-derived lapping features dominate 96\% of tree decisions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item `lap_out_recur` (+0.5397 gain share): High recurrence of identical transaction amounts sent to counterparties.
    \item `lap_out_payees` (+0.4200 gain share): Fan-out smurfing of structured amounts across multiple destination mules.
    \item `time_since_prev_src`: High-frequency transactional bursts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Dataset 3 (SAP W\"urzburg) Attribution}
In SAP W\"urzburg, relational accounting document types dominate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item `tx_type_Materialzugang` (SHAP +1.16): High-risk goods receipt postings.
    \item `account_type_D` (SHAP +0.48): Customer/vendor balance line classifications.
    \item `fan_in` (SHAP +0.30): Clearing accounts receiving mass payroll allocations.
\end{enumerate}
